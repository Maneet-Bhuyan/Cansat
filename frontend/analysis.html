MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS

Aerospace Formulations & Kinematics Rigor

How our post-flight pipeline transforms raw, noisy barometric and inertial measurements into publication-grade flight dynamics without introducing lag or numerical artifacting.

Zero-Phase Smoothing Digital Signal Processing

Savitzky-Golay Polynomial Filter

Why it matters: Standard rolling average or low-pass filters introduce a delay of 1.2 to 2.5 seconds. In a rocket flight, a 2-second delay means parachute ejection triggers when the rocket is already hurtling downward. Savitzky-Golay fits a local moving polynomial, cleaning jitter with zero phase lag.
y_j = ∑_{i=-m}^{m} C_i · x_{j+i},   N = 2m + 1 = 11,   p = 2

A convolution window of length $N=11$ with a $2^{\text{nd}}$-order polynomial preserves peak apogee curvature while stripping high-frequency pressure quantizations caused by atmospheric acoustic turbulence.

Impact Dynamics Kinematic Profiling

Vertical Velocity & Dynamic G-Shock

Why it matters: Raw GPS vertical velocity lags and jumps erratically. By differentiating our smoothed altitude curve with respect to temporal time steps ($dt = 0.8\,\text{s}$), we isolate clean climb and descent speeds alongside absolute 3D resultant impact shock.
v_z(t) = \frac{dz_{\text{smooth}}}{dt},   |G|(t) = \sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2 + a_z^2}

The resultant acceleration magnitude immediately identifies high-G booster burnout shocks, explosive parachute ejection jolts, pendulum swings, and terminal ground impact.

Thermodynamics Atmospheric Physics

ELR & Poisson Potential Temperature

Why it matters: As air rises, it expands and cools under lower pressure. Potential temperature (θ) reveals the temperature a parcel of air would have if brought adiabatically to standard sea-level pressure ($P_0 = 1000\,\text{hPa}$), instantly uncovering thermal inversion layers.
\Gamma_{\text{ELR}} = -\frac{dT}{dz} \left[\frac{^\circ\text{C}}{100\,\text{m}}\right],   \theta = T \cdot \left(\frac{P_0}{P}\right)^{\frac{R_d}{c_p}}

Using MetPy formulations ($R_d / c_p = 0.286$ for dry air), we diagnose atmospheric boundary layer stability and locate thermal inversion caps (demonstrated in Scenario 07).

Aerodynamics Descent Regime

Parachute Drag & Time-to-Touchdown

Why it matters: Dividing altitude by speed assumes constant velocity. But as the CanSat descends, air density increases from $1.10\,\text{kg/m}^3$ to $1.225\,\text{kg/m}^3$, causing continuous parachute deceleration according to classical aerodynamic drag theory.
v_{\text{term}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 m g}{\rho(z) \cdot C_d A}},   \rho(z) = \frac{P(z)}{R_d \cdot T(z)}

Combined with our Multi-Quantile Gradient Boosting regressor, the system predicts Time-to-Touchdown with calibrated P10, P50 (median), and P90 uncertainty bounds.
